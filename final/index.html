Bioinformatics: The Intersection of Biology and Computer Science

Bioinformics is the application of computer science, statistics, and mathematics to the collection, storage, analysis, and interpretation of biological data, such as DNA, RNA, and amino acid sequences. This discipline has become increasingly important in the era of big data, as it allows researchers to make sense of the vast amounts of information generated by modern biological experiments. Due to innovations in sequencing technologies and laboratory methods, biological data is being generated at an unprecedented scale from multi-omics experiments, such as genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics, and other omics fields. As of 2025, EMBL-EBI, the largest biological data repository in the world, stores approximately 123 petabytes (10^15 bytes) of raw sequence data. The use and development of bioinformatics tools and algorithms is essential for extracting meaning from broad patterns and raw data and for advancing our understanding of biology, medicine, and evolution.

Bioinformatics had its beginnings in the 1960s, when research groups, such as those led by Dr. Margaret Dayhoff, first began compiling collections of protein sequences and developing computerized algorithms to analyze and compare them. Once RNA and DNA could be sequenced by the late 1970s, bioinformatics expanded to include the analysis and storage of nucleic acid sequences.

This website focuses on the four largest fields within the discipline: genomics (the study of DNA), transcriptomics (the study of RNA), proteomics (the study of proteins), and metabolomics (the study of metabolites - small molecules which are the products of cellular processes).
